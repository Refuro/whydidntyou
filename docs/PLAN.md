# Plan v3.6: controlled measurement of solver behavior relative to a reference library and a documented training distribution

Status: DRAFT v3.6 for a ninth review (source inspection and record fixtures). Adversarial sandbox probes are run by the author and shipped as regressions (the reviewer's platform halts that activity). Earlier versions and all reviews with their probe scripts are under `docs/archive/`. Nothing in Phase 2 or 4 has been run. §12 lists changes from v3.5; §13 lists earlier changes.

## 0. What v3 claims, and what it explicitly does not

Three propositions are kept separate everywhere in this project:

- **(P-exposure)** no document in the audited training corpus explicitly demonstrates the target solution family;
- **(P-language)** the submitted solution's behavior lies outside a formally specified restricted language L0;
- **(P-capability)** the target capability was absent from the trained model before testing.

P-exposure and P-language can be established. **P-capability is not implied by them and is not claimed anywhere in this project.** A model can learn, during training, a procedure that no training document spells out.

**Claim A (Phases 1–2):** on a specified task distribution and under specified budgets, frontier models do or do not match an independently challenged, frozen solver portfolio; results are reported as performance and resource comparisons plus a semantic analysis of the submitted programs.

**Claim B (Phase 4):** a model trained from random initialization on a fully documented, hash-pinned synthetic corpus satisfying P-exposure for a withheld solution family does or does not produce solutions satisfying P-language on withheld-family tasks, compared causally with a matched model whose corpus includes that family. Reported as controlled exposure and generalization, never as invention.

The four-level ladder (parameter search / method selection / method synthesis / representation synthesis) is retained only as informal vocabulary in the discussion section.

## 1. Axes of measurement

1. **Validity.** Task performance on sealed evaluation pairs; for invariant tasks, a validated conserved quantity, scored by a fixed prediction rule, separately from the model's own classifier.
2. **Relationship to the reference portfolio** — two separate reports: (a) *performance* vs the portfolio (§2) on the same worlds; (b) *semantic analysis* of the submitted program: L0 non-membership (§4.2 defines L0 semantically; a witness pair certifies that a quantity is OUTSIDE L0; absence of a witness means *unknown*, never membership), primitives used, and whether a registered portfolio member's behavior matches it on challenge pairs. Accuracy equality is never taken as method identity.
3. **Resources.** Model tokens, sandbox executions, wall-clock, labeled examples used.
4. **Reuse** — one of three registered kinds, never conflated: (R1) additional sealed pairs in the same world; (R2) transport under a supplied symbol/algebra isomorphism to a fresh world; (R3) a frozen *constructor* (procedure) re-fitting a quantity from a fresh world's rules under a registered adaptation budget, compared against fitting from scratch. The literal fitted quantity is expected to fail on fresh worlds and that failure is not reported as a failure of abstraction.

## 2. The reference portfolio

Membership (each with a registered primitive language and compute budget; one total budget per member, stated):
1. modular linear algebra over symbol counts;
2. finite-group homomorphism search into Z_m, S3, Q8, D4, A4, with and without quotient coarsening;
3. **finite-monoid constraint synthesis** (SAT/SMT/CSP over symbol images or a bounded multiplication table, with closure, associativity, identity, rule equations and labeled examples as constraints);
4. **joint coarse-feature classifier** over the tuple of all registered quotients and count features (not each separately);
5. finite-state inference that tracks the full string-product state (bound justified against the algebra size: ≤ q^q states for a transformation monoid on q points), plus rewrite normalization / canonical forms;
6. **constraint-guided search in a registered program grammar** driven by relations and counterexamples (not pure enumeration);
7. nuisance-feature classifiers (length, histogram difference, edit distance, LCS, positional agreement, short-pattern counts) and bounded bidirectional search, as scored baselines;
8. brute-force BFS under the sandbox budget.

Two portfolio scores are reported: **oracle portfolio** (best member per world chosen with sealed labels — labeled as an oracle) and **implementable selector** (member chosen from public rules and training pairs only). Members are never modified using sealed scores. Before freezing, a solver author who did not design the generator attacks development worlds under the registered budgets; the portfolio is frozen only after that challenge. Coverage table, all evaluated worlds, failure modes and resource curves are published. A general-distribution result and any library-resistant challenge set are reported separately. A null comparison is not evidence of non-superiority unless a registered equivalence margin is met. **B2 budget decision (2026-11-03):** the B2 BFS node/transition limits and sandbox budgets are the starting registration. The independent B4 challenger may propose increases; changes must be recorded before the portfolio freeze, with none after it.

**B4 author decisions (2026-11-03):** `invariantGame-v2-b3` is within library: the finite-monoid CSP solves 1,720/1,720 accepted audit worlds and the implementable public selector solves 1,719. This is not a defect; Claim A compares model and portfolio performance and resource cost on this class. No expansion is made now. After the independent challenge, a separately registered library-resistant challenge family may be added by dated addendum and reported separately, without altering this family. Positional-template successes are retained data: the checker must refuse the historical positional convention, the template remains a portfolio/oracle member, and amended rates by effective family are reported alongside the 16/16 historical score. Builder 4x/16x BFS settings remain resource curves, not frozen budgets. Freeze requires both the independent challenge report and B4 review, followed by registration of accepted methods and budgets; B5 then B6 follow that freeze.

## 3. Phase 2 — hidden-invariant pair classification (Claim A)

### 3.1 Task semantics
Pair classification on a stated promise distribution: positives from random rule walks (connected by construction); negatives with different invariant value (disconnected by construction). Same-invariant disconnected pairs are outside the distribution and the paper says so. Two distributions are reported: **natural sampling** and a **quotient-challenge** distribution (§3.4), each with its own sealed pairs.

### 3.2 Generator soundness and verification
Admitted families are monoid homomorphisms from strings to a finite monoid M: count vectors mod m; products in finite abelian groups; products in registered non-abelian groups; products in small transformation monoids. **Conservation is proved, not sampled:** verify M's closure, identity and associativity exhaustively on its table; define I as the fold of symbol images; check every rule equation I(u)=I(v) in M. The homomorphism property then gives I(xuy)=I(xvy) for all contexts. No short-context cutoff is used as a proof. Two sound extensions are admitted only with their augmented summaries: alternating weighted sums carrying (length parity, alternating sum), and fixed-pattern counts carrying (count, prefix, suffix, capped length); rules must preserve the full summary, and these families are audited for rule coverage and shortcut creation before use.

Per world, an **identifiability audit** reports: number of inequivalent representations consistent with the exposed rules and training pairs; reachable-state occupancy; rule coverage; the reference solver's information requirement. Non-identifiable worlds are construction defects: exclude them from the assigned set at construction time, record seed and reason `non-identifiable` in the defect ledger, and never reroll them. The full identifiability audit remains attached to the defect.

### 3.3 Families and the transfer family (custody: disclosed-knowledge fallback, see below; §9–10 use the same wording)
F-count, F-abelian, F-nonabelian, F-monoid as in v2. For every generated world the **generated submonoid** is checked for non-commutativity and the reachable word support for exercising it; a world whose images happen to commute (e.g. all powers of one 3-cycle) is classified as abelian regardless of the ambient algebra's name.

**Transfer family — disclosed-knowledge fallback.** The reviewer declined custody (an author-visible workspace cannot provide author-blindness). Unless an external collaborator who did not design the generator takes custody under the salted-commitment procedure in the v3 review (§2, custody), the paper uses the fallback: the author's knowledge of F-sealed is disclosed; the family, sampler, portfolio, scaffold and protocol are frozen in one commit before any confirmatory outcome is observed; a single freeze manifest (source commit plus content hashes of family/generator, sampler, portfolio, scaffold/prompts, model settings, budgets, seed procedure, exclusion/retry policy, statistical endpoints, and a declaration that it is the active confirmatory freeze) is bound by an independently verifiable external timestamp (an RFC 3161 timestamp-authority receipt, or an OpenTimestamps proof verified as *completed* before the first confirmatory run); every confirmatory batch records the manifest digest; amendments get a new manifest and timestamp before affected runs; all started batches and superseded freezes are disclosed. The result is reported as *instance* and *family* transfer under disclosed designer knowledge, never as blind family selection. A public Git date is not external time evidence.

**B2 algebra decision (2026-11-03):** the implemented 2–4-letter representation class and small transformation-monoid subclass (dihedral permutations plus constants on three or four points) are sufficient for B3 and the B4 portfolio challenge. Expansion to larger alphabets or full transformation monoids is allowed only by a dated amendment to `prereg/invariantGame.md` before the freeze, and only if the B4 portfolio solves every registered family; never after confirmatory outcomes are seen.

### 3.4 Negative sampling (replaces v2's universal quotient requirement, which is infeasible for e.g. C2×C2×C2)
1. **Feasibility first.** For each admitted algebra, solve the finite feasibility problem: support points are unequal value pairs (nonidentity differences for groups), constraints are registered quotients (normal subgroups) or congruences (monoids). Report infeasible cases; do not regenerate indefinitely.
2. **Witness pairs.** For separation from count-based representations, include balanced pairs with identical symbol histograms and different labels (e.g. `…ab…` vs `…ba…` under non-commuting images). This is a certificate that histogram-only classifiers are bounded on the challenge distribution; the bound is computed, not assumed.
3. **Joint coarse-feature bound, correctly denominated.** The challenge distribution is validated against the *optimal* feature-only classifier over the joint coarse features (§2.4), computed on an enumerated bounded support of labeled string pairs by the weighted feasibility program in the v3 review (§11.3): nonnegative pair weights with positive and negative mass 1/2 each, per-class caps t_z ≥ max(positive mass, negative mass) in class z, Σ t_z ≤ τ. This bounds the best feature-only classifier's balanced accuracy, not one fitted classifier. Negative-only accuracy is not used (an always-negative classifier attains 100% on it). **Applicability by family:** for F-count and F-abelian the invariant is a function of counts, so a histogram challenge is inapplicable by construction; these families are controls and are reported as "feature-separable". The challenge is required only for families whose generated submonoid is non-commutative.
4. **Nuisance matching.** Positive/negative challenge pairs are matched on length, histogram distance, edit distance and LCS; the nuisance audit (§3.5) is re-run after conditioning.
5. **Calibration vs sealed.** Instance acceptance uses calibration pairs; sealed evaluation pairs are generated afterwards and never used for acceptance.
6. **Order and feasibility.** The bound in (3) is recomputed after nuisance matching (4) and after rounding to finite counts, or matching is imposed inside the same feasibility program. The infeasibility claim concerns the **registered bounded string-level problem** (eligible strings and lengths, certified positives by rewrite path, certified negatives by invariant inequality, the exact joint feature map, τ, weights, tolerances, matching mode). Non-commutativity of the generated submonoid is an eligibility condition, not evidence that the bound and matching are jointly feasible; an infeasible support is reported, never regenerated until the failure disappears.

**B4r support decision (2026-11-03; supersedes B4):** the registered distribution is the **matched/padded challenge**, sampler `sealed-matched-padded-v2`. Construction takes the first representative per label in each joint-feature/nuisance class, selects at most 32 matched classes in seeded order, and duplicates each short core with a neutral prefix. For K selected classes there are 4K support points: uniform weights 1/(4K) exactly satisfy the registered label, joint-feature and nuisance balance constraints and attain the max-min objective (no minimum weight can exceed the average); an analytic oracle checks the simplex answer. Rounded multiplicities and distinct support counts are reported per world. The fully realized challenge and natural lists are independently permuted using domain-separated PRF streams. The large rows are dependent copies of short cores: the small/large gap estimates **padding robustness**, not size generalization. A distribution with genuinely longer cores requires a separate future registration. Defects certify failure on this registered bounded support, not impossibility over all strings. A challenger proposing a different support must hold the registered nuisance constraints fixed; dropping them does not count as a ceiling counterexample.

### 3.5 Leakage audit (as v2, unchanged in substance)
Metadata-only, canonicalized-rule and labeled-pair predictors of family; nuisance-feature predictors of label with a registered equivalence margin (upper 95% bound < 55% on fresh worlds); portfolio with rules-only / examples-only / both; presentation interventions. Structural recognizability is reported as portfolio coverage, not hidden.

### 3.6 Prompt and output conditions
Two paraphrases. Unrestricted vs quantity-requested output; the request is an intervention and is reported as one. Unrestricted evaluates the classifier only. Quantity-requested requires the classifier plus either `quantity(state)` or declarative `quantityFold`, never both. Functional quantities receive bounded empirical conservation with total surrounding-context length ≤3; only the declarative fold route is proved.

## 4. Phase 4 — controlled exposure and generalization (Claim B)

### 4.1 What is withheld
The withheld family is **non-commutative structure**: no training document contains a solution, trace, explanation or failed attempt whose quantity is order-sensitive. (v2's wording "no group-valued object" was wrong: abelian groups are in-toolkit.) In-toolkit families: F-count and F-abelian.

### 4.2 L0 and its certificate
L0 is defined **semantically**: quantities that depend on the input only through its symbol histogram, q(s)=f(count(s)), with f ranging over the in-toolkit expression grammar. A proof by induction over the grammar establishes that every L0 expression is invariant under symbol permutation within a string. **Certificate that a submitted quantity is outside L0:** two strings with identical histograms and different outputs (`ab` vs `ba` suffices when images do not commute). **Certificate that a task requires information beyond L0 at the threshold:** the relevant feature for pair classification is the ordered pair of full histograms h(a,b) = (count(a), count(b)). Within a world, group sealed pairs by z = h(a,b); with n_z+ and n_z− the label counts, the maximum accuracy of any classifier that sees only z is U_hist = 1 − Σ_z min(n_z+, n_z−)/N. If a fraction α of the weighted set lies in balanced conflicting classes then U_hist ≤ 1 − α/2 (equality only when the remaining mass has no conflicts); the sufficient condition is registered as α ≥ 20% for a ceiling ≤ 90% under a 95% threshold, and U_hist itself is always computed exactly on the sealed set. **Sealed sets are constructed, not sampled:** a population-level ceiling does not survive independent sampling into a finite set (with many feature classes, a 100-pair sample almost surely contains one label per class and its empirical ceiling is 100%), so sealed pairs are drawn as matched conflict blocks with predetermined label and weight quotas per pair-feature class, and the realized U_hist is verified below the registered threshold before, and independently of, any model result. Classifier score, conflict fraction and ceiling use the same weights; with example weights w, U_hist = 1 − Σ_z min(W_z+, W_z−)/W where W_z± are class label masses and W the total (the count formula is the equal-weight case); quotas realize integer multiplicities, tolerances and zero-weight handling are registered, and an independent checker recomputes the ceiling from the exported examples rather than trusting a solver status. **Both bounds on the sealed set:** the histogram ceiling and the joint coarse-feature bound (§3.4) are each recomputed on the final sealed examples after block construction, matching and rounding; a histogram conflict block is not assumed to be a joint-feature conflict block. **Interface:** the classifier is evaluated as a pure per-pair function; pair order is randomized and administrative identifiers are withheld, and a frozen quantity's outputs are tested for invariance under reordering and relabeling. **Acceptance vs verification:** sealed pairs are never used to accept an instance (§3.4.5); the realized-ceiling check on them is a fail-closed assertion of the construction, and a failure is reported as a generator defect under a registered disposition rule (world excluded from the denominator with its seed logged) — never redrawn until it passes. Matching histograms *within* each pair is insufficient (positive (ab,ab) vs negative (aabb,abab) are separable); conflicts must be within pair-feature classes (positive (ab,ab) vs negative (ab,ba)). The ceiling is exact for the finite sealed set and is computed per world. Both certificates are one-sided and assume a pure, deterministic, resource-bounded quantity evaluated against a frozen world; the submitted quantity's conservation is verified separately (bounded empirical check unless a proof artifact is supplied) and the certificate is searched within the task domain, tied to the quantity that achieved sealed success above U_hist. Neither certificate speaks to P-capability.

### 4.3 Corpus and exposure audit
- Solutions, traces, explanations and failed attempts are **emitted from a typed AST** in a fixed formal notation and rendered to text; the AST grammar for in-toolkit documents cannot express order-sensitive quantities. No post-hoc text filtering is relied on.
- Generic transition-table evaluators (`state = T[state][sym]`) are **excluded from in-toolkit documents**, since they evaluate non-commutative products regardless of the tables shown. Sequence-processing competence is taught by registered documents that are disclosed as part of the exposure intervention.
- Input rule systems in in-toolkit documents are themselves in-toolkit (commutative) and are audited.
- The exact delivered token stream, tokenizer, generator code, configuration, curriculum and packing rules are hashed; random initialization; no fine-tuning, validation-driven selection, or test-time examples outside the audit. Unique underlying worlds and structural equivalence classes are reported, not only token counts.
- Audit levels: generation/syntax (every document parsed and certified), semantics (L0 proof + search for alternate encodings in inputs and labels), actual exposure (hashes), interpretation (P-exposure and P-language only).

### 4.4 Interface frozen before any training
Executable output language (restricted; its primitives disclosed as exposure), tokenizer, context length sufficient for the full task prompt plus attempt history, interaction protocol, verifier. If the output language cannot express order-sensitive programs, P-language success is impossible by construction and the experiment is void; the language therefore includes general bounded loops and tables, and their presence in training documents is part of the audited exposure.

### 4.5 Staged execution with registered gates
1. Audit the generator; produce the L0 proof and witness certificates.
2. Overfit a tiny diagnostic set to catch interface bugs.
3. **Benchmark throughput** (tokens/s, peak memory) at the intended context length, precision and optimizer on the actual GPU before choosing token budgets. The v2 "hours to two days" figure is withdrawn; the plan uses measured numbers.
4. Train one small **target-withheld** model and one **matched target-exposed** model (same tokens, steps, architecture, decoding, budgets; data replaced, not added).
5. **Gates**, set before inspecting withheld-family results, per retained seed, on ≥100 fresh development worlds per control family: ≥90% world-level success with 95% lower bound >80%; ≥99% syntactically valid submissions. Controls: (C1) in-family synthesis on unseen F-count/F-abelian worlds with new presentations and the same prompt burden; (C2) the target-exposed model must solve unseen withheld-family worlds; (C3) representation-supplied diagnostic — the withheld model is given the missing representation and tested on fresh cases (diagnoses execution vs representation; does not establish prior absence).
6. Only if gates pass: the size × seed grid, with every seed reported individually (three seeds are three models, not a population) and corpus generation either fixed or independently replicated, stated.

**Registered null event and margins.** The withheld-model endpoint is the world-level rate of *certified out-of-L0 success* (sealed success above U_hist plus a validated conserved quantity with a witness), reported separately from raw task success. Under one checkpoint, the registered seed-sampling approximation, and independent provider draws, zero certified successes in n fully resolved worlds bounds the rate at 1 − 0.05^(1/n) (one-sided 95%): 8.9% at n=32, 2.95% at n=100; excluding a 5% rate needs n ≥ 59. **The measure:** probability of certified success for a fixed checkpoint, budget and decoding protocol, over the registered world-generating distribution *conditional on calibration eligibility and successful sealed-set construction*, with the **same construction-valid world set used for the exposed and withheld arms**. **Construction failure and evaluation failure are different events:** construction-valid worlds whose evaluation is interrupted (request failure, unfinished job) are retried under the registered policy (R retries); if still unresolved they are reported as *unresolved* and are neither successes nor failures. **Resolved-only counting does not identify the target.** Selecting resolved evaluations measures P(success | C, E), which equals the target P(success | C) only under a missingness assumption (resolution independent of potential outcome) that disclosure does not establish; difficult or long-output worlds may systematically fail to resolve. The registered analysis therefore has two layers (`src/bounds.js`): (1) a **completeness gate** — the primary population claim is made only if every assigned construction-valid world resolves under the retry protocol below; (2) if the gate fails, the reported bound is the **conservative** one that treats every unresolved world as a possible success (nominal one-sided 95% hypergeometric numerical reference obtained by inversion for count s + u, M = 2^32 and n assigned construction-valid slots within each repeat stratum; the binomial reference is shown alongside it. This is a registered approximation, not an exact fixed-K identity for stochastic decoding; e.g. 10 unresolved of 100 with zero observed successes gives 16.4%, not the resolved-only 3.3%), and the resolved-only figure is shown beside it only with its assumption stated. For paired exposure contrasts, unknown per-world outcomes are likewise allowed to vary (bounds), not dropped. **The endpoint is success of the slot procedure (success in the terminating completed run).** **Author decisions, 2026-11-03:** the primary stratum is repeat 0, one binary slot-procedure outcome per seed; repeats 1..r are exploratory within-world variability and never pooled as worlds. Eligibility is a deterministic function of the seed, so the eligible population M_C is a fixed subset of the sampling population M = 2^32. For n ≤100 and M_C ≥10^6, the registered approximation uses the M = 2^32 figure at the printed precision of 0.01 percentage points. When proposal/eligible counts permit a plug-in M_C estimate, report both numerical references and identify the estimate and the assumed lower bound explicitly; a point estimate does not establish M_C ≥10^6. The registered potential outcome is the slot-procedure outcome under the registered protocol with provider randomness, treated as an independent Bernoulli draw per seed. Averaging products of the seeds' failure probabilities over a uniform sampled subset gives P(zero) ≤ (1 − mean p)^n. This justifies the fully resolved zero-success bound; it is not a fixed-K identity and does not by itself establish positive-count coverage. **Retry protocol:** an interrupted evaluation is retried up to R = 2 times as a *fresh* solver run (the interrupted history is archived, not resumed); each retry is a new run id bound to the same slot; a request that may have produced an unreceived response consumes its retry; a solver that exceeds its registered execution budget is a *solver failure*, not an infrastructure event; unresolved after R retries is reported as unresolved. The assignment is fixed (same construction-valid world set and arm set, fixed draw count) before any outcome; the stopping rule is "all assigned slots terminal (resolved or unresolved after R retries)"; no resolution-based continuation. Counts of proposed, calibration-eligible, construction-valid, resolved and unresolved worlds are reported per family with every excluded seed and its reason. The defect ledger is written before any solver runs and retained when a batch is refused; a generator change after the freeze is a new freeze. The confirmatory n per checkpoint, the effect margin for the exposed-vs-withheld contrast, and the handling of unknown certificates, invalid code, timeouts and API failures are fixed before the withheld-family results are inspected. Exposure-helper documents (copying, indexing, reversing, bounded loops) are classified by a published allowed/rejected AST list; generic table evaluators are rejected. "Per retained seed" never licenses silent filtering: every trained seed and every failed gate is reported. C2/C3 contexts and weights are isolated from the unprompted target condition.

A failed diagnostic run is engineering evidence, not a negative result.

## 5. Interventions and process measures

- **Hints:** prompted-restart estimand by default (hint present from attempt 1 in a fresh run), named as such; a continuation estimand (fork the identical failed history into randomized arms) is a separate experiment. Arms interleaved in a seeded random order within world; equal budgets; both controls reported; selected on development worlds, confirmed on fresh worlds; rung categories (content-free / strategy / solution-family) registered.
- **Post-mortems:** descriptive only. Runs archive the exact system prompt, the exact initial request, every raw response and the exact verifier feedback text (run-log schema 5; post-mortem records are schema 5 too); the retrospective record is assembled from those archived pieces only — ending with the final verifier feedback — and given in full to both the responder and the judge, with the reveal as a separate message so no archived content is dropped; it is labeled with how it differs from the solver's own view under limited memory. Evidence spans currently cite raw responses only; claims about the task prompt or verifier feedback are therefore rated unverifiable until typed evidence spans for those record kinds are added (registered follow-up). The judge extracts one claim about the model's own submitted attempts and rates it supported / contradicted / unverifiable; the label is recorded as **provisional** (`humanReview: pending`) because substring validation establishes provenance only — scope compliance (prompt- or feedback-dependent claims must be unverifiable) and entailment are settled by the registered blinded human review, whose annotation set includes prompt-dependent, feedback-dependent and response-supported controls with violation rates reported; claim and evidence must be exact character-for-character substrings (offsets recorded; no whitespace normalization, since a removed newline can change code semantics); an unverifiable rating must carry no evidence; any validation failure yields `unresolved`. Categories are multi-label with "unknown". No causal endpoint. Solver, responder and judge model identities are recorded separately. Exact spans establish provenance, not entailment; entailment is adjudicated by humans.
- **Observable computation:** counters in the execution interface (sandbox executions, candidate evaluations where the model uses a provided tester); coverage limits stated; blind descriptive annotation with mixed/unknown.
- **Scaffold transfer:** developed on F-count/F-abelian/F-nonabelian development worlds, frozen, tested on fresh worlds and on F-sealed; reported as instance transfer plus family transfer with the strategy-leak caveat.

## 6. Phase 1 — search-breadth control
**Verifier (all phases).** Submitted programs run in a WebAssembly-hosted interpreter (QuickJS via `quickjs-emscripten`), one fresh WASM instance per side. The host provides no imports, and the interpreter is created **without the Date intrinsic**, so inside the sandbox there is no filesystem, process, environment, network or clock (the v5 review showed a timing channel through a retained Date constructor; the capability probe now checks three clock paths). The interpreter stack limit (64 KB, about 300 JS frames, stated in the model's contract) is set below the WASM stack so native recursion (JSON, parsing) overflows inside the sandbox; every host-boundary exception is caught and returned as a bounded failure, so a submitted program cannot abort the attempt loop. Timeouts use the interpreter's interrupt handler, which is cooperative; a hard wall-time budget, if promised, requires an external supervisor and is not currently claimed. Every boundary value is a string built inside the sandbox and parsed by the host. `selftest` runs a ten-capability positive-control probe (expected all absent) plus nineteen reproduced channels and hang vectors from reviews 2–5. **Randomness policy (audited on the pinned build):** `Math.random` in a fresh QuickJS runtime is not deterministic across instances — observed: two instances created 1.2 s apart produce different sequences; upstream QuickJS seeds from `gettimeofday`, which is the likely cause and has not been verified in the WASM build's source — so before any submitted code runs, `Math.random` is replaced by a fixed-seed xorshift128 generator and locked non-writable/non-configurable; `selftest` compares a 512-bit output vector against an independent host execution of the registered recurrence whose SHA-256 is pinned in the freeze (so a coordinated edit of both copies cannot silently redefine the sequence), asserts identical sequences across instances created at different times, and checks the descriptor flags and that assignment and redefinition are ineffective. The claim is: *no channel from encoder to decoder other than the counted bitstring is known; the clock intrinsic is absent and the randomness source is deterministic and locked, both verified by executed tests on the pinned build*; residual risk is an unaudited intrinsic path or a memory-safety escape from the WASM sandbox. "We import nothing" is a necessary condition, not the claim. Successful value computation is deterministic from canonical state (no clock, fixed locked RNG); completion vs failure is governed by a wall-clock interrupt and is handled separately as evaluation validity. That is a *precondition* for §4.2's witness certificate, not its enforcement: a stateful program can still return different values on repeated calls. Purity is enforced by the Phase 2 quantity-evaluation interface (to be implemented before Phase 2 runs): each evaluation of a candidate quantity runs in a fresh canonical sandbox state (fresh instance, fixed RNG state), both witness strings are evaluated under that contract, and a timeout or invalid evaluation is an *invalid evaluation*, never counted as a differing output. The k-curve is unchanged. All existing runs are exploratory. The confirmatory k-curve is frozen at tag `confirm-phase1` (to be created at the commit that freezes `experiments/kcurve.json`; the tag's hash is reported).

## 7. Phase 3 — appendix, gated (unchanged from v2)

## 8. Statistics and reporting
- Unit: world. Repeat 0 is the primary stratum (one binary slot-procedure outcome per seed); subsequent repeats are exploratory within-world variability, analyzed separately and never pooled as worlds. Effort: success-by-budget curves and censored time-to-success; no conditional medians.
- **One primary contrast** per experiment at 95%. Any further contrasts are **exploratory**: nominal 95%, no family-wise control, labeled as such (this resolves the v3.1 §8/§12 contradiction; the exploratory policy is the registered one). Intervals: paired world bootstrap; when every world difference is identical the bootstrap is withheld as uninformative and inference is recorded as unresolved pending a registered bounded-outcome fallback. Per-condition population intervals are not reported.
- **Seed domain and world identity.** Generators accept only integer seeds in [0, 2^32−1] (larger values alias smaller ones in the 32-bit RNG state; the v5 review showed seeds 1 and 2^32+1 generate the identical world). Every instance carries a content digest (family, generator version, seed, options, sample, test); trial rows and run logs record it. Coincident semantic worlds are retained draws and are reported. Repetition of one world is recorded as repeats, never as a second seed.
- **One typed contract** (`src/validate.js`, schema 5) is enforced by the **writers** (`run.js` validates every meta, attempt and end record before appending it; `experiment.js` validates every trial row before appending it and validates its configuration before any request) and by both **readers**. Inputs are normalized once at the command line (`normalizeRunInput`: seed domain, integer budget, memory enum, registered option keys) and stored fields are asserted without coercion. Result records are typed variants (a successful result must carry finite scores; a failed one a registered stage and error string); messages are shape-checked; the single success event is *derived from validated attempt results* and must agree with the end record and with early stopping. **Interruption is a distinct state:** a request failure writes an `end` record with `status: "interrupted"` and a reason, leaves its pre-transport `dispatched` attempt archived with unknown response/usage, is refused by post-mortems, and is recorded by `experiment.js` as a `slot-execution` in an append-only chain with executionIndex 0..2; only interruption authorizes a fresh restart, while completion or setup failure terminates the slot. Exactly one `slot-terminal` references the full chain (foreign-batch, missing-link, duplicate, and post-completion records are refused), is listed by the reporter in a missingness ledger that is written *before* any inferential refusal, and is never scored by any consumer (`hints.js` counts interrupted and setup-failed jobs separately and scores only completed runs). **Legal states only:** attempts never exceed the budget; an interruption can occur only before a success and before exhaustion; a completed failed run has exhausted its budget. **Scores are arithmetic, not fields:** bits per symbol and both ratios must equal what the authoritative bit count, sequence length, baseline and oracle imply. **One configuration validator** (`validateConfig`) runs in the writer before the batch header and first request — the whole grid, version pins, policies, contrasts, memory modes and registered option domains per key — and in the reader; a configuration the reader would refuse cannot start a batch.
- **World identity, two digests.** `instanceDigest` is provenance (includes the seed label). `worldDigest` is semantic (family, generator version, effective parameters including the hidden secret, and both data arrays; seed label excluded; defaults canonicalized). The reporter **regenerates** each trial's instance from (seed, options) and refuses any row whose generator version, digests or secret differ from the regenerated ones; strata (hidden k / L) use the regenerated secret, never the copied field; every summary row is **bound to its run log** (the log is re-validated and must agree on run id, status, success, attempts used, digests, seed, domain, model, memory and options; the row is a cache of the log, not evidence). It refuses repeats of one seed with differing `worldDigest` and paired conditions with identical options whose per-seed worlds differ. **Full binding:** each run log is bound to (a) the regenerated instance — the log's own n/baseline/oracle are checked against it and every attempt's arithmetic is re-validated against the *regenerated* references, never the log's fields; (b) the configured treatment — hint text, scaffold path and scaffold content hash (hashed at preflight and recorded in the batch header), memory, options, budget, model; (c) the execution slot — runs record `{batchId, condition, seed, repeat, executionIndex, runId}` at execution time and the reporter requires an exact match, and one run id can serve one slot only (independent slots are independent executions). Each `slot-execution` has status `completed`, `interrupted`, or `setup-failed`; interrupted logs are bound identically, and completed runs cannot be relabeled unresolved. Run IDs are 16 random bytes in hexadecimal. Requests are reconstructed byte-for-byte from regenerated instances and archived history using the batch-pinned prompt-builder hash; system/scaffold text and feedback are checked. Named token counters and seconds are recomputed from archived usage across the whole chain, with missing usage reported as unknown. **Preflight** reads and hashes every scaffold file and exercises every condition's generator on every seed before the batch header and first request. Relative option constraints are checked on effective values (defaults resolved), identically in the normalizer, generators and config validator.
- **Sampling design (registered, matches the code).** A confirmatory batch's seed list is produced by the registered draw (`src/draw-seeds.js`): n distinct seeds produced by a PRF-style expansion of a master; this is a computational approximation to simple random sampling without replacement under the assumption that the SHA-256 expansion is indistinguishable from independent uniform 32-bit outputs; the master for a confirmatory batch is taken from a prospective public randomness source (a NIST Randomness Beacon pulse or a drand round) at a registered time after the freeze timestamp, so the list cannot be chosen after examining candidates. The exact master text and count are recorded as `seedDraw: {master, n}`; `validateConfig` recomputes the draw and refuses a config whose list differs, and refuses a confirmatory config with hand-picked seeds. For fully resolved zero-success observations with independent provider Bernoulli draws per seed, average the products of failure probabilities over the sampled eligible subsets: P(zero) ≤ (1 − mean p)^n. No exact fixed-K identity is claimed for provider randomness; §4.5 registers the eligible-population approximation and sensitivity figures. Two distinct seeds may still generate the same semantic world; such coincidences are **retained as separate draws and reported** (ledger and report), never excluded. No bound on their probability is claimed: the seed→world map is not proven injective, and for some admitted configurations it is far from it (e.g. `hiddenPeriod {A:8, p:1, L:1}` has at most eight worlds); coincidence rarity is not needed for the binomial argument, only the retention policy.
- **Manifest contract.** The reporter validates the configuration (unique seeds and condition ids, positive-integer budgets, strict-boolean coupling flags, pinned generator versions per domain) and every trial against it (condition, seed, repeat range, domain, options, memory, model, batch, generator version, budget, and one success event: null or an integer within the budget, consistent with attempts used; failed trials must have exhausted the budget). The **missingness policy** is a config field: `refuse` (default; an incomplete batch is not analyzed) or `exploratory` (read-out labeled development-only, with a machine-readable list of every missing trial). Mixed batches are refused. Batches are resumed only through the validated resume path, with source/config identity, prior-state digest, archived receipts, original execution indices and budgets preserved. Inconsistent batches are abandoned with a durable disposition. `sharedWorld` is a declared assumption; a generator-specific coupling test (shared latent-draw digest across options) is registered per domain before any contrast across options is reported.
- Sample: ≥32–40 worlds per primary condition; power simulated from pilot estimates for the hierarchical design.
- Annotation: two qualified reviewers on every claimed out-of-L0 solution; blind to model and outcome (family cannot be blinded when code reveals it — stated); control artifacts included.
